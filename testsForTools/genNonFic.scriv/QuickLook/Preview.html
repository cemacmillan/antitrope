    • But where indeed?

      But where, indeed?


      Not in the clay.

      Not in the glaze.

      Not in the gentle wavering line where the potter's wheel hesitated -

      that little quiver that tells you he had a stone in his shoe.


      All of these are revealed to a certain reader of the vase, and begin to tell us a story before the fingers make contact.


      Then, it must be that the glyphs are part of the story of the vase, and, they are understood by the exploration of its surface.
